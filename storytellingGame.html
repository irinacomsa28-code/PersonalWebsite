Serious Game for Social Impact
| bachelor thesis LMU Munich x USI Lugano

What I Did

  • Designed in Figma & developed in Unity C# a serious game to mitigate gender stereotypes in young children
  • Conducted a Critical Design Session with the help of my supervisor, Irene Zanardi, along with Prof Dr. Monica Landoni and 4 other researchers
  • Conducted and analyzed two user sessions and iterated based on the collected data
  • Validated game effectiveness by designing a research-backed reflection coding scheme and conducting data analysis

Keywords

  • Child-Computer Interaction

Collaborators

For my bachelor thesis, I designed and implemented a serious game for children that encourages reflection on gender stereotypes. Socio-political digital tools are a heavily underexplored area within Child–Computer Interaction (CCI) despite their high potential for social change. Developed as part of a collaboration between LMU Munich and USI Lugano (Erasmus university), the project combined academic research with hands-on design and development. The game was designed in Figma, built in Unity (C#) and iteratively refined through continuous research, discussions with CCI researchers and two user sessions with 9 primary-school children from Lugano, Switzerland.
